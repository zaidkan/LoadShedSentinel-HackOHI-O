# Reproducible response-time counterfactual

`engine.response_comparison.compare_response(inputs)` compares the same repair job under two declared information/coordination workflows. This is a `counterfactual_simulation`, **not measured Sentinel savings**. `benchmark_response()` exposes the identical cases/grid to the API; `python scripts/benchmark_response.py` writes `data/processed/response_benchmark.json` without dependencies or random sampling.

Each branch computes `max(qualified_ready_hours, shared_blocker_ready_hours) + shared_repair_hours`. Readiness is sequential diagnosis + coordination + optional analysis overhead; the wrong-package branch adds a declared **non-overlapping coordination/availability wait**. It does not add travel/setup/safety already counted in the shared 4.5-adjusted repair workload. Concurrent access/upstream requirements form one shared readiness gate; use their maximum rather than sum. The repair/staffing/job are unchanged.

The expected value enumerates correct/wrong-package branches **before** applying their user-assumed probabilities. A shared gate can mask the apparent benefit, so `max(expected_readiness, gate)` is not the calculation. The difference of expected durations needs no assumption about cross-arm wrong-package correlation. We do not infer the distribution of individual causal benefits.

Inputs accept finite nonnegative scalar hours or `[lower, central, upper]`, ordered throughout. Probabilities are scalar `[0,1]` assumptions. The returned lower/upper savings values are an assumption envelope over independent arm-specific time extremes with a common blocker; they are not confidence intervals or calibrated quantiles. Shared repair uncertainty cancels. A declared sufficient-evidence flag enables the proposed assisted arm; unknown/insufficient falls back to the baseline and receives zero credit. This flag is caller-supplied, not an accuracy certification. Detection lead is a separate output and never becomes prevented-outage hours.

The default UI-compatible illustration uses a four-hour conditional extra wait, assumed repeat-dispatch probabilities of 20% versus 10%, a quarter-hour assisted verification overhead and three common repair hours. Expected durations are 4.8 versus 4.65 hours: **0.15 hours (nine minutes)** of modeled net benefit. Equal probabilities give −0.25 hours instead. None of these inputs is an empirical default.

A separate evidence example uses `(0.20 - 0.10) × 8 = 0.8 expected hours`, with one hour of correct-package readiness and 2.25 common repair hours. Its rates and eight-hour conditional extra wait are **assumed**, not fitted to AEP. An additional quarter-hour verification reduces this example to 0.55 hours. A 12-hour shared blocker gives zero; an assumed assisted wrong-package rate of 30% versus baseline 10% gives **−1.6 hours**. The conditional eight-hour case is one deliberately declared extreme, not an average claim.

The 96-row sensitivity grid varies baseline/assisted wrong-package probabilities, two extra-wait choices and three shared gates. With no shared gate and equal correct-package readiness, break-even is `(p_baseline - p_assisted) × extra_wait > assisted_overhead`. The illustrative 0.25-hour overhead and eight-hour penalty require a probability difference greater than 0.03125; this is algebra, not a validated target.

See [time_savings_evidence.md](time_savings_evidence.md) for primary-source analogues and the field study needed for a measured claim. The supplied ARCOS fields contain calendar dates, blank crew/job linkage and no dispatch/arrival/re-energization timestamps. They cannot establish empirical hourly response or restoration benefit.

Validation: `python -m pytest -q tests/test_response_comparison.py`. Checks include hand arithmetic, branch-before-bottleneck expectation, common-repair invariance, shared-bottleneck zero, negative assisted outcomes, abstention, separate lead time, finite/range/probability rejection and an independent grid of attainable interval inputs.
